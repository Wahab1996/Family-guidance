Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) – Clinical Reference

Based on: GAD Presentation (Dr. Aisha Almohammadi, PGY‑1, supervised by Dr. Adel Alharbi, 17 Dec 2025). [file:21]

OVERVIEW

Overview / التعريف العام

Definition (English)

Generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) is defined in DSM‑5 as excessive anxiety and worry occurring more days than not for at least 6 months about multiple events or activities, causing significant distress or functional impairment. [file:21]

It most commonly presents as excessive worrying about minor issues, often with somatic symptoms and pathological behavioral changes (e.g., sleep disturbance, fatigue, muscle tension, irritability, difficulty concentrating). [file:21]

التعريف (عربي)

اضطراب القلق المعمّم هو حالة يتميّز فيها الشخص بقلق مفرط وصعوبة في التحكم بالقلق حول عدة مجالات من الحياة، يحدث في معظم الأيام لمدة لا تقل عن ٦ أشهر، ويسبب ضيقًا واضحًا أو تدهورًا في الأداء الوظيفي أو الاجتماعي. [file:21]

غالبًا ما يظهر على شكل قلق مستمر حول أمور يومية بسيطة، مترافق مع أعراض جسدية وتغيّرات سلوكية مرضية مثل اضطراب النوم، التعب، التهيّج، توتر العضلات، وصعوبة التركيز. [file:21]

Epidemiology & Burden / الوبائيات

Saudi Arabia

  • National survey: lifetime prevalence of GAD ≈ 1.9% in the general adult population. [file:21]
  • Higher prevalence in specific groups (e.g., university students) – reported rate 12.7% in one study. [file:21]
  • Female gender and younger age are associated with higher prevalence and symptom severity. [file:21]

Lifetime Occurrence

  • ≈ 1% of Saudi males diagnosed with GAD at some point in life. [file:21]
  • ≈ 3% of Saudi females diagnosed with GAD at some point in life. [file:21]

GAD in Primary Care

  • Anxiety disorders are among the most common mental health problems in primary care. [file:21]
  • Patients with anxiety disorders are about twice as likely to have other chronic medical conditions and present with somatic symptoms. [file:21]

Who to Screen?

  • Adults 8–64 years, including pregnant and postpartum persons (USPSTF – Grade B). [file:21]
  • Children and adolescents 8–18 years for anxiety disorders (Grade B). [file:21]

Diagnostic Criteria / معايير التشخيص

DSM‑5 Criteria (English)

  • Excessive anxiety and worry about multiple events/activities (e.g., work, school) on most days for ≥6 months. [file:21]
  • Difficulty controlling the worry. [file:21]
  • Associated with ≥3 of the following (≥1 in children) on most days for ≥6 months:
    • Restlessness or feeling keyed up/on edge.
    • Being easily fatigued.
    • Difficulty concentrating or mind going blank.
    • Irritability.
    • Muscle tension.
    • Sleep disturbance. [file:21]
  • Clinically significant distress or impairment in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning. [file:21]
  • Not attributable to substances (e.g., drugs) or another medical condition (e.g., hyperthyroidism) and not better explained by another mental disorder (panic disorder, PTSD, somatic symptom disorders, etc.). [file:21]

ملخص معايير DSM‑5 بالعربي

  • قلق مفرط وقلق توقّعي حول أكثر من مجال (العمل، الدراسة، الصحة...) معظم الأيام لمدة ≥ ٦ أشهر. [file:21]
  • صعوبة في التحكم بالقلق أو إيقافه. [file:21]
  • وجود ≥ ٣ أعراض (عرض واحد في الأطفال) من:
    • التململ أو الشعور بالتوتر أو “على أعصابه”.
    • التعب بسهولة.
    • صعوبة التركيز أو شرود الذهن.
    • التهيّج.
    • توتر العضلات.
    • اضطراب النوم. [file:21]
  • أن تؤدي الأعراض إلى ضيق ملحوظ أو ضعف في الأداء المهني أو الاجتماعي أو غيره. [file:21]
  • ألا تكون الأعراض ناتجة عن مادة أو حالة طبية أخرى أو اضطراب نفسي آخر. [file:21]

Differential Diagnosis / التشخيص التفريقي

Major Psychiatric Differentials

Disorder Key Features
Panic disorder Recurrent unexpected panic attacks; no obvious trigger; attacks last minutes; persistent concern/fear of attacks for ≥1 month. [file:21]
Social anxiety disorder Marked fear/anxiety about social situations or performance in public, with symptoms lasting ≥6 months. [file:21]
Specific phobia Persistent intense fear of particular objects or situations; duration ≥6 months. [file:21]
Separation anxiety disorder Excessive fear, anxiety, or avoidance of separation from attachment figures, often after stressful life events; normal under age ~3 years. [file:21]

Medical & Substance‑Related Causes

  • Hyperthyroidism: heat intolerance, excessive sweating, weight loss, insomnia, palpitations, goiter, tachycardia. [file:21]
  • Medication adverse effects: benzodiazepine withdrawal, anticonvulsants, opioids, sedatives, anticholinergics, beta‑agonists, corticosteroids. [file:21]
  • Substance use / withdrawal: alcohol withdrawal, excessive caffeine, nicotine, opioid withdrawal, stimulant (e.g., amphetamine) intoxication. [file:21]
APPROACH

Clinical Approach / النهج السريري

Stepwise Approach (English)

  1. Consider GAD in patients with positive screening and/or medically unexplained physical symptoms. [file:21]
  2. Perform a complete history and physical examination. [file:21]
  3. Assess for psychiatric and medical comorbidities, including substance use. [file:21]
  4. Order diagnostic tests as needed to rule out organic causes (e.g., TFTs, urine drug screen). [file:21]
  5. Confirm diagnosis using DSM‑5 criteria. [file:21]
  6. Evaluate suicide risk. [file:21]

History of Present Illness – Key Items

  • Chief complaint, onset, course, duration of symptoms, and impact on daily functioning. [file:21]
  • Associated physical symptoms: fatigue, muscle tension, tremor, palpitations, abdominal discomfort, plus red flags like chest pain, syncope, severe palpitations. [file:21]
  • Emotional and cognitive symptoms: feeling on edge, irritability, impaired concentration, negative thoughts. [file:21]

عناصر القصة المرضية والفحص

  • التاريخ الطبي: أمراض مزمنة، فرط نشاط الغدة الدرقية، حالات قد تُسبب أعراض القلق. [file:21]
  • التاريخ الدوائي: أدوية مثل الكورتيكوستيرويدات، β‑agonists، مضادات الاختلاج، البنزوديازيبينات (انسحاب). [file:21]
  • التاريخ النفسي والأسري: اضطرابات نفسية في العائلة، سوء استخدام مواد. [file:21]
  • التاريخ الاجتماعي: الأداء الدراسي/الوظيفي، أحداث ضاغطة، التدخين، الكحول، المخدرات. [file:21]

تقييم الانتحار والاضطرابات المصاحبة

  • أسئلة مباشرة: «هل راودتك أفكار بإيذاء نفسك؟» «هل فكرت يومًا في إنهاء حياتك؟». [file:21]
  • تحرّي الاكتئاب والذهان: «كيف مزاجك؟» «هل تشعر بأن أفكارك ليست أفكارك؟» «هل تسمع أو ترى أشياء لا يراها الآخرون؟». [file:21]

Mental State & Physical Examination

  • Typical MSE: well‑groomed, slightly tense posture, “on edge” anxious mood, restricted affect, logical but worry‑dominated thought processes, catastrophizing content, mild concentration impairment, intact judgment, fair insight. [file:21]
  • Physical exam: often normal unless comorbidities; pay attention to tachycardia, nervousness, thyroid enlargement; request thyroid function tests and urine drug screen if indicated. [file:21]

Management / العلاج

Management Framework

Clarification & Reassurance (Arabic Patient Education)

اضطراب القلق العام هو حالة تستمر لفترة طويلة، يشعر فيها الشخص بالقلق في مواقف مختلفة، سواء دون سبب واضح أو نتيجة ضغوط بسيطة، وقد يسبب أعراضًا نفسية وجسدية مثل التوتر المستمر، صعوبة التركيز أو النوم، والدوخة أو خفقان القلب. [file:21]

يشمل العلاج: العلاج النفسي (مثل العلاج السلوكي المعرفي) والأدوية لتخفيف القلق، ومع العلاج المناسب يمكن لمعظم الأشخاص السيطرة على القلق والعيش بصورة طبيعية. [file:21]

Lifestyle & Non‑pharmacologic Advice

  • Regular exercise.
  • Good sleep hygiene.
  • Avoid caffeine, alcohol, and recreational drugs.
  • Smoking cessation reduces short‑ and long‑term anxiety symptoms. [file:21]
  • Yoga, mindfulness meditation, and relaxation strategies. [file:21]

Psychotherapy – CBT

  • CBT can be as effective as pharmacotherapy (evidence grade A) for mild to moderate symptoms. [file:21]
  • Core components: patient education, self‑monitoring, cognitive restructuring, exposure therapy, relaxation training, and relapse prevention. [file:21]

Pharmacologic Therapy – General Principles

  • Consider medication when symptoms are moderate to severe, when psychotherapy is unavailable/unacceptable, or when response to CBT is poor. [file:21]
  • SSRIs and SNRIs are the mainstay of pharmacotherapy for GAD. [file:21]
  • Continue medications for at least 6–12 months to minimize relapse risk. [file:21]

SSRIs

Onset of effect: usually delayed 2–4 weeks or more; common adverse effects include nausea, diarrhea, insomnia/agitation, somnolence, sexual dysfunction, hyponatremia. [file:21]

Citalopram 10 mg 10–40 mg Can prolong QT interval at higher blood levels. [file:21]
Fluoxetine 10–20 mg 20–60 mg More insomnia/agitation; long half‑life; minimal withdrawal if stopped. [file:21]
Paroxetine 10–20 mg 20–50 mg Mildly sedating, weakly anticholinergic, withdrawal if not tapered. [file:21]

Benzodiazepines

  • Rapid onset and effective in reducing acute anxiety symptoms. [file:21]
  • Associated with withdrawal, rebound anxiety, and dependence; therefore not recommended as first‑line and should not be used long term. [file:21]
  • Used short‑term (e.g., lorazepam 1 mg/day in divided doses) for severe anxiety or to manage early SSRI adverse effects such as agitation and insomnia, especially in patients without a history of substance use disorder. [file:21]

Stepwise Pharmacologic Management (Inadequate Response to First SSRI)

Simplified from the flowchart in the presentation. [file:21]

  1. Adequate trial of first‑choice SSRI.
  2. If partial response → augment with buspirone and monitor 4 weeks.
  3. If full response → continue current treatment.
  4. If no response or insufficient response:
    • Stop buspirone, consider augmentation with gabapentin and monitor 4 weeks.
    • If still inadequate, stop augmentation agents and switch to a second SSRI. [file:21]
  5. If major depressive symptoms are prominent (dysphoria, anhedonia, appetite changes), consider a non‑SSRI antidepressant such as mirtazapine or vilazodone. [file:21]
  6. In patients without problematic alcohol/substance use, options may include hydroxyzine, a short benzodiazepine trial, or second‑generation antipsychotics (e.g., aripiprazole, quetiapine) as augmentation in resistant cases. [file:21]

Children & Adolescents – Anxiety Disorders

  • CBT should be offered to patients 6–18 years with social anxiety disorder, GAD, separation anxiety disorder, specific phobia, or panic disorder (Level A evidence). [file:21]
  • SSRIs should also be offered to this age group for these anxiety disorders (Level A). [file:21]
  • Combined treatment (CBT + SSRI) may be more effective than either alone in some cases (Level B). [file:21]

Supplements & Botanicals / المكملات العشبية

Ashwagandha

  • Recent trials (2024–2025) suggest significant reduction in stress and anxiety and lower cortisol at doses 240–600 mg/day in stressed adults. [file:21]
  • Appears well‑tolerated short‑term (up to ≈3 months); long‑term safety is unknown. [file:21]
  • Contraindicated in hormone‑sensitive prostate cancer and pregnancy (risk of preterm labor); may increase sedation with benzodiazepines. [file:21]
